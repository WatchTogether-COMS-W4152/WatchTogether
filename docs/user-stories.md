# User stories

This branch delivers thirty web application stories: group management (US01–US11), group voting (US12–US15), a shared watchlist with watched/unwatched status (US16–US18), TMDB discovery, metadata, transfers, and recovery (US19–US22), episode progress tracking (US23–US26), and spoiler-safe episode discussion (US27–US30). Each story has a stable Cucumber tag so it can be demonstrated and tested independently.

Acceptance criteria for **US19**:

- Both add-title forms suggest movie and TV results from the current text. The dropdown identifies the title, type, year, poster, score, and description; unrelated people and adult results are excluded.
- Keyboard users can browse with arrow keys, select with Enter, and dismiss with Escape. Typing a new query cannot let an older response replace the new results or the selected title.
- Selecting a result saves its catalogue identity and canonical details. Editing the text clears the selection. Invalid selections and attempts to forge title metadata create no partial entry.
- Different adaptations and movie/TV namesakes can coexist. The same TMDB movie or TV show cannot be added twice to the same destination in one group; other groups can make their own choices.
- Signed-out visitors must sign in. Outsiders and removed members cannot search, preview, or add titles for the group, even by reusing a saved URL.

Acceptance criteria for **US20**:

- Selecting a search result previews its details; saved voting and watchlist entries retain them across requests and for other members.
- TMDB scores and private group voting scores have distinct labels. Age certifications identify the US region; missing age information is never inferred from the audience score or another country's certification.
- Missing posters, years, descriptions, and ratings have explicit fallback text. They do not prevent adding an otherwise valid title.
- Existing plain-text records, votes, and watched states survive the metadata migration. The app does not guess a TMDB identity for old titles.

Acceptance criteria for **US21**:

- Any current member can add any suggestion, including another member's plain-text suggestion, to the same group's watchlist. New entries start unwatched.
- Copying preserves metadata and leaves the suggestion, individual votes, total score, and rating count intact. It uses saved data even when TMDB is unavailable.
- Repeated clicks and concurrent duplicate additions leave one entry. A title already on the watchlist keeps its watched state, and voting shows **On watchlist**.
- Removing the suggestion preserves the watchlist entry. Removing the watchlist entry makes the still-existing suggestion available to add again.
- A suggestion cannot be copied through another group's URL; outsiders and removed members cannot transfer it.

Acceptance criteria for **US22**:

- Short queries make no external request; unmatched searches show no matches. Loading, no matches, and failures have distinct feedback.
- Authentication, rate-limit, network, malformed-response, and deleted-title failures produce useful feedback without exposing credentials or raw upstream errors.
- An unsuccessful selected-title addition creates no incomplete record. The member can retry after recovery or enter a plain-text title in either destination.
- Saved lists, voting, and suggestion transfers remain usable during a catalogue outage. Failed lookups are not cached as successful titles.

## Episode discussion user stories

Every comment belongs to one episode of a show on the watchlist. A member sees the text of a comment only when their own episode progress has reached that episode (or when they wrote it); for later episodes they see only how many comments are waiting. This rule is enforced on the server: hidden comment text and authors are never sent to the browser.

| Story                                            | User need                                                                                                                                                                           | Executable coverage                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **US27 — Discuss an episode with my group**      | As a member, I can post a comment on a specific episode of a show so friends who have watched it can read my reaction. Comments are grouped by episode, oldest first, with my name. | `@US27`, [episode_discussion.feature](../features/episode_discussion.feature); [EpisodeComment model](../spec/models/episode_comment_spec.rb), [discussion requests](../spec/requests/episode_comments_spec.rb), [component tests](../app/frontend/test/episode-discussion.test.tsx). |
| **US28 — Read the discussion without spoilers**  | As a member who is behind, I see only comments on episodes I have watched, plus a count for later episodes, so I can join in without spoilers. Catching up unlocks them.            | `@US28`, [episode_discussion.feature](../features/episode_discussion.feature); visibility model specs and request specs asserting hidden text is absent from the response.                                                                                                            |
| **US29 — Only comment on episodes I've watched** | As a member, I can't post about an episode beyond my own progress, so I can't spoil it for others by mistake. The form starts at my current episode and explains any rejection.     | `@US29`, [episode_discussion.feature](../features/episode_discussion.feature); validation model specs, request specs, and form error component tests.                                                                                                                                 |
| **US30 — Remove a comment**                      | As the author of a comment, or as a group owner, I can delete it after confirming. Other members cannot delete someone else's comment.                                              | `@US30`, [episode_discussion.feature](../features/episode_discussion.feature); permission request specs and confirmation component tests.                                                                                                                                             |

Acceptance criteria for **US27**:

- Any current member can comment on an episode they have reached. Season and episode are whole numbers of at least 1; the comment is trimmed and must contain 1–1000 characters.
- Episodes are ordered by season, then episode (S1 E10 comes before S2 E1). Comments within an episode are oldest first and show the author's display name, avatar, and a short time.
- Outsiders, removed members, and signed-out visitors cannot read or post comments, and a show cannot be reached through another group's URL. Movies have no episode discussion.

Acceptance criteria for **US28**:

- A member with no recorded progress sees no comment text, only counts. A member exactly on an episode can read that episode's comments.
- For each later episode the page shows "N comments on S2 E5, unlocked when you get there." It contains neither the text nor the author of those comments, in the HTML or the Inertia JSON.
- Moving progress forward unlocks the comments immediately. A member always sees their own comments.

Acceptance criteria for **US29**:

- Posting on an episode beyond the member's progress is rejected with an explanation naming their current episode; nothing is saved. A member with no progress is told to mark episodes as watched first.
- Blank and overly long comments are explained and not saved. Posting is rate-limited per member.

Acceptance criteria for **US30**:

- The author and any group owner see a delete control; deletion asks for confirmation and can be cancelled. Other members receive 403 and the comment remains.
- Deleting a show deletes its comments. If an author's account is deleted, their comments remain and appear as from a former member; if a member is removed from the group, their comments remain under their name.

Run all acceptance stories with `bundle exec cucumber`, one story with `bundle exec cucumber --tags @US19`, or all catalogue stories with `bundle exec cucumber --tags @tmdb`. Cucumber sends real HTTP requests into Rails using separate cookie jars for different viewers. Scenarios are isolated with database transactions and cleared rate-limit state. TMDB scenarios substitute only the external HTTP boundary with deterministic responses and a fake credential; the actual TMDB client, application controllers, and models run normally. No live TMDB account or network is needed. These service acceptance scenarios do not execute JavaScript. React component tests and the manual browser demonstration cover the dropdown, keyboard handling, stale responses, confirmation, and cancellation separately.

RSpec also checks details that are not useful as user-facing story steps: database uniqueness, role constraints and foreign keys, rollback of failed group creation, password/session digests, cookie settings, CSRF handling, throttling, and excluded sensitive fields. The corresponding request suites are [authentication](../spec/requests/authentication_spec.rb), [profiles](../spec/requests/profiles_spec.rb), [groups and invitations](../spec/requests/groups_spec.rb), [membership management](../spec/requests/memberships_spec.rb), [suggestions](../spec/requests/suggestions_spec.rb), [ratings](../spec/requests/votes_spec.rb), and [watchlists](../spec/requests/watchlist_items_spec.rb); model constraints and the voting ranking are tested under [spec/models](../spec/models). TMDB-specific RSpec tests additionally check canonical identities, database constraints independent of validation, safe network failures, cache isolation and expiry, credential non-disclosure, and duplicate transfer races. SimpleCov includes every application controller, model, and service, including unloaded files, and enforces at least 90% line coverage independently for RSpec and Cucumber. Separate reports live in `coverage/rspec/index.html` and `coverage/cucumber/index.html`; [verification.md](verification.md) records measured runs and their scope.

Acceptance criteria for **US23**:

- Season and episode must be whole numbers of at least 1. Missing, zero, negative, decimal, or non-numeric values are explained next to the field and leave my previous progress unchanged.
- Saving again replaces my position, including moving backwards; each member has one position per show.
- **Watched next episode** moves me on one episode within the same season, or to S1 E1 if I had not started.
- Progress persists and is visible to other members. Movies have no episode progress.

Acceptance criteria for **US24**:

- Every current member appears once, furthest ahead first (season before episode, so S2 E1 is ahead of S1 E10). Members who have not started appear last as **Not started**. Ties are ordered by name.
- Everyone at the furthest recorded position is marked **Caught up**. Nobody is caught up before anyone has started.
- Member emails and invitation codes are never included.

Acceptance criteria for **US25**:

- The next episode is the one after the position of the member furthest behind. While any member has not started, it is S1 E1.
- When everyone is at the same episode, the page says everyone is caught up and names the following episode.
- Season lengths are unknown, so the next episode stays in the same season; members move to a new season by entering it.
- When a member leaves or is removed, their progress no longer affects the list, caught-up status, or next episode.
- The watchlist shows **Next together** for shows someone has started.

Acceptance criteria for **US26**:

- Clearing removes only my progress for that show; other members and other shows are unchanged. Clearing when I have no progress is harmless.

For US23–US26, signed-out visitors must sign in, outsiders and removed members receive not found, and a show cannot be reached through another group's URL.

## Two-user demonstration

To demonstrate US19–US22, follow the account/group setup below and complete these checks before deleting the temporary group:

1. With TMDB configured locally, type **Interstellar** in voting. Observe search feedback and the poster, year, score, and movie/TV labels. Use the arrow keys and Enter to select the 2014 movie, then check the selected details and US certification before adding it.
2. Rate that suggestion **Love it** and choose **Add to watchlist**. Confirm it remains ranked with the same score and rating count, and its button now reads **On watchlist**. The other member should see the same watchlist metadata without seeing who voted which way.
3. In the watchlist, search **Severance** and distinguish the TV show from the same-named movie. Select the TV show, add it, and confirm both the series and movie are present after refreshing. Mark one watched and check the other member sees the new state.
4. Remove the copied Interstellar suggestion and confirm its watchlist entry and watched state remain. On a second suggestion, remove only its watchlist entry and confirm the voting page offers **Add to watchlist** again.
5. Edit an input quickly, including trailing spaces, and confirm its dropdown follows the latest text. Check Escape dismissal, missing-poster fallbacks when present, and narrow-screen usability. No-match and outage recovery are deterministic in the tagged acceptance and component tests; the live catalogue's current results and ratings may vary.

To demonstrate US23–US26, add a plain-text show such as **Severance** to Friday Night's watchlist and open its title:

1. **Bob:** Enter season 1, episode 3 and save. Choose **Watched next episode** and confirm Bob is at S1 E4. Try season 0 and confirm the error leaves S1 E4 in place.
2. **Alice:** Refresh the title. Bob is listed first as **Caught up** and Alice as **Not started**; the next episode for everyone is S1 E1. Record S1 E2 and confirm the next episode becomes S1 E3 and the watchlist shows **Next together: S1 E3**.
3. **Bob:** Choose **Clear my progress**. **Alice:** Refresh and confirm Bob is **Not started** and Alice is now caught up.

Use the running app in two separate browser profiles or one regular and one private window. Use temporary demo accounts with different email addresses. Do not use a production database for destructive testing.

1. **Alice:** Open the app and choose **Create account**. Register with a display name, email, and password. Confirm the groups page opens automatically and displays its empty state.
2. **Alice:** Open **Create group**, submit a blank name to see validation, then create **Friday Night**. Confirm Alice is the owner and appears once in the member list.
3. **Alice:** Copy the group's invitation code. Keep this original code for step 8.
4. **Bob:** In the second browser session, register another account. Open **Join group**, try an invalid code, then paste Alice's code. Confirm Friday Night opens with both names.
5. **Bob:** Return to **My groups** and reopen Friday Night. Confirm Bob sees the member list but no invitation code or reset control. Join with the same code again and confirm Bob still appears once.
6. **Bob:** Open **Profile**, change the display name, refresh, and confirm the new name remains. Refresh Alice's group page and confirm the member list uses Bob's updated name.
7. **Bob:** Sign out. Confirm visiting `/groups` or `/profile` returns to sign-in. Sign in again and confirm the group membership remains.
8. **Alice:** Replace the invitation code. Confirm it changes and Bob remains a member. **Bob:** Try joining with the saved original code and confirm it is rejected; joining with the new code succeeds without a duplicate membership.
9. **Bob:** Create a separate group. Copy its URL. **Alice:** Open that URL while signed in and confirm a not-found page protects Bob's private group.
10. Refresh both sessions and inspect browser/server logs for unexpected errors. Confirm the layout remains usable at a narrow browser width.
11. **Alice:** Open **Manage** on Bob's row and choose **Make owner**. Read the permissions explanation and cancel; Bob should remain an ordinary member. Repeat and confirm. Both names should now have an **Owner** badge. **Bob:** Refresh Friday Night and confirm invitation and management controls appear. **Alice:** Confirm you remain an owner too.
12. **Alice:** Open **Manage** on Bob's row and choose **Remove from group**. Confirm the warning names Bob, identifies his owner role, and explains that the invitation will change. Cancel and verify both members remain. Save the current code, repeat, and confirm removal. Bob should disappear from Friday Night, and the code should change. There should be no **Manage** control on Alice's own row.
13. **Bob:** Refresh Friday Night and confirm access is denied. Confirm Friday Night is absent from **My groups**, Bob's separate group remains, and the saved code is rejected by **Join group**. **Alice:** Share the new code. **Bob:** Join again and confirm you return as an ordinary member without invitation or management controls.
14. **Alice:** Open **What should we watch?** on Friday Night. Submit a blank title to see validation, then suggest **Paddington** and **Cats**. Try suggesting **paddington** again and confirm it is rejected. Rate Paddington **Love it** and Cats **Maybe**. **Bob:** Open the same page, rate Paddington **Maybe** and Cats **Not interested**, then change Cats to **Maybe**. Confirm Paddington ranks first with +3 and Cats shows +2, each "2 of 2 rated", and that Bob's page highlights only Bob's own ratings. **Bob:** Confirm there is no **Remove** control on Alice's titles. **Alice:** Remove Cats, cancel once, then confirm, and check it disappears for both viewers.
15. **Bob:** Open **Our Watchlist** on Friday Night. Submit a blank title and check the field error, then add **Paddington** and **Cats**. Confirm both start **Not watched**. Return to the group and reopen the watchlist; the entries should persist. **Alice:** Open the same watchlist and confirm both titles appear. **Bob:** Add a title to his separate group's watchlist. **Alice:** Try that watchlist URL and confirm access is denied.
16. **Bob:** Mark Paddington **Watched**. **Alice:** Refresh and confirm the shared status, then mark it **Unwatched**. **Bob:** Refresh and confirm it is back to **Not watched**. Both owners and ordinary members can update any watchlist entry.
17. **Bob:** Choose **Remove** for Cats and cancel; Cats should remain. Repeat and confirm. **Alice:** Refresh and confirm Cats is gone and Paddington remains. Watchlist removal is available to every member, including entries another member added.
18. **Alice:** On the temporary Friday Night group, choose **Delete group**, then cancel and confirm the group remains. Open the confirmation again and confirm deletion. Both viewers' group lists should lose Friday Night; its previous URL, watchlist URL, and invitation should no longer work. Both accounts and Bob's separate group and watchlist should remain available.

The account, group, membership, and watchlist changes persist in the configured database. Browser storage alone is not the source of truth. Session-expiry timing and replay of a revoked cookie are covered by automated tests rather than waiting 30 days during the demonstration.

Group management intentionally separates shared ownership from leaving or demotion. Owners cannot remove themselves, and there is no owner-demotion control in this iteration. Removing someone is not a permanent ban: sharing the replacement invitation allows them to join again as an ordinary member.
